## Findings and worked examples

1. **Confirmed semantic defect:** investment assignment can satisfy the target before any investment transfer. Payroll retirement can be omitted from the same target. Savings assignment can satisfy its target even if the assigned money was spent. This follows directly from the formulas; it is not a claim that every historical assignment was untransferred.
2. **Observed local discrepancy:** 2026 through September has $94,793.64 of `N`, a $14,219.07 investment target, $10,030.04 of investment assignments, and `investments_on_plan = false`. Payroll employee retirement is $12,500.10. Nine cleared YNAB Investments-category transactions net to $20,030.04, but their destination-account receipt has not been verified. Using these transactions as a provisional taxable-contribution candidate would yield $32,530.14 employee retirement + taxable, demonstrating why the current flag cannot answer the ticket's primary question. In 2024, investment assignments were $11,533.34 while Investments-category net outflow was $1,533.34; this also shows why assigning cannot prove investing. All figures came from read-only local DuckDB queries.
3. **Observed savings discrepancy:** 2022 Savings + Emergency Fund assignments were $5,792.58, and `savings_on_plan = true` against $3,558.90, while their combined net change was $0. In 2025, $5,790.36 assigned passed $4,932.49, but combined net change was $2,100 and the Savings category itself had $0 net growth. The current signal measures allocation intent, not durable saving.
4. **Income issue:** `N` includes all YNAB Income-group net activity, including any paystub reimbursement deposit and other inflows, while payroll retirement and HSA contributions were already deducted before net pay. Thus the four percentages sum to 100% of deposited income but do not describe the allocation of all employee-controlled compensation. The owner counts employee HSA contributions as retirement saving; HSA assets and reimbursable receipts must not be counted again as liquid savings. [IRS Publication 969](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p969) distinguishes HSA contributions and distributions.
5. **HSA expense classification:** The owner says HSA-eligible expenses paid from the budget should count as Needs, with any later HSA reimbursement tracked separately. The local snapshot confirms `HSA Items for Reimbursement` is mapped to `Needs`, with $1,660.10 of 2026 net outflow in 16 transactions (`combined.transactions` joined to `cleaned.category_groups`). This is a current YNAB mapping, not a hard-coded SQL rule. No change is needed to its current Needs mapping; the future reimbursement must not reclassify the original expense or become new earnings.
6. **Waterfall issue:** `actual_rollover = N − Needs spend − Wants spend − Savings assignments − Emergency Fund assignments − Investments assignments` (`monthly_adherance_inputs.sql:100-105`). The yearly waterfall then allocates available cash to apparent savings/investment gaps as *capacity* (`yearly_adherance_waterfall.sql:207-240`, `:288-300`); gap coverage does not document a transfer or change a contribution flag. The worksheet hides those gap columns (`adherance_waterfall.py:105-136`). ETH-622 must label actual flows, potential reallocations, and closing stocks separately.

**Counterexample (dollars, illustrative):** A paycheck has $10,000 gross cash earnings, $2,500 taxes/required benefits, $1,500 employee 401(k), $200 employee HSA, and $5,800 bank deposit. Add $400 recurring eligible nonpayroll income and $100 reimbursement: bank inflow is $6,300, and the source identity is `$10,000 + $400 + $100 = $2,500 + $1,500 + $200 + $6,300`. The current $6,300 basis gives a $945 investment minimum and $315 savings minimum. Assigning $1,000 to Investments without transferring it and assigning then spending $400 of Savings passes both current tests. Under the proposed broader basis, `D = $5,800 + $400 + $1,500 + $200 = $7,900`, excluding reimbursement. At an *illustrative* 25% long-term-savings minimum, actual 401(k) + HSA is $1,700 and no taxable deposit leaves a $275 shortfall; a verified $1,000 taxable deposit makes $2,700 and passes. At an *illustrative* 3% unrestricted-cash minimum, retained cash $0 leaves a $237 shortfall. These percentages are scenarios, not an approved policy. If the $400 were a one-off, core `D` would be $7,500 and the $400 would enter the separate surplus allocation path.

## Alternatives and recommendation

| Approach | Income denominator and numerator | Benefit and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Keep retirement outside | Verified non-reimbursement net income to budget accounts; taxable *deposits* for the investment test; employee 401(k) and HSA shown separately; taxes and payroll deductions excluded. | Internally consistent if the label explicitly means a taxable-only target. It hides whether overall employee long-term saving is sufficient and requires separate retirement policy. Current assignment-based actuals would still need correction. |
| **Recommended: one employee long-term-savings minimum** | `D = verified recurring bank income excluding reimbursements, transfers, and irregular inflows + employee payroll 401(k) and HSA contributions on that income`; actual = employee 401(k) + employee HSA + verified new taxable deposits − withdrawals/distributions from these accounts, with internal moves netting to zero. Employer match/returns excluded. Taxes and non-saving payroll deductions are outside `D`. | Directly answers whether new employee dollars remained in long-term accounts. A broader denominator raises dollar targets for unchanged percentages; HSA funds are limited to eligible uses, so show their portion separately. Rebuild targets from this definition. |
| Separate retirement/HSA and taxable minima | Same `D`; distinct employee 401(k), HSA, and taxable contribution actuals/targets. | Shows account purpose and liquidity goals clearly, but fixed subtargets can declare failure despite a high total contribution and require an explicit allocation preference. Use a split as a secondary guide if desired. |
| Gross-cash allocation | All gross cash earnings plus eligible other income; taxes, required deductions, HSA, retirement, taxable deposits, and spend each get a distinct line. | Best complete paycheck bridge, but taxes and benefits consume part of 100%; more data classification and changing tax rates make a simple fixed 50/30/15/5 rule misleading. Use as reconciliation display, not the primary controllable-spending denominator. |

For the recommended model, call the combined retirement/investment target **met only when confirmed net new employee money retained in long-term accounts during the period meets the target for recurring eligible income earned in that same period**. Count pre-tax, Roth, and after-tax employee 401(k) contributions and employee HSA contributions from paystubs once; include other retirement deposits only with a source and dedupe key; count taxable net new deposits reaching investment accounts. Subtract withdrawals/distributions to spendable cash, including HSA reimbursements, and exclude sales, market gains, internal account transfers, and employer match. Show gross 401(k), HSA, taxable deposits, and distributions separately so the net amount is auditable. Use YNAB outflows as provisional evidence until reconciled to destination deposits. A refund/reversal reduces the numerator. If a $200 HSA contribution is reimbursed to the bank and the same $200 is deposited in brokerage, the net long-term addition is `$200 − $200 + $200 = $200`, not $400. Show actual and unverified candidate amounts separately; missing evidence yields `unknown`, not an automatic pass. Existing paystub parsing and component audits are useful and need no redesign solely for this decision.

For liquid savings, the owner defines success as a minimum increase in **unrestricted** cash reserves measured by the YNAB `Savings` category's net balance growth, with opening/closing balances, assignments, spending, and internal category moves reconciled. A new assignment alone is insufficient evidence. Show the Emergency Fund balance and top-ups separately; do not make moving the same dollar between Savings and Emergency Fund count twice. Keep HSA balances outside liquid savings even though employee HSA contributions count in the combined long-term target. The IRS treats HSA contributions and qualified distributions as distinct events ([IRS Publication 969](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p969)).

| Proposed period fact | Definition and treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Grain and signs | One calendar month plus annual/YTD rollups; positive inflows, contributions, withdrawals, and spend, with signed net results. Keep source/pay date and bank posting date to explain timing residuals. |
| Core denominator `D` | Verified recurring after-tax bank income, excluding transfers/reimbursements/irregular inflows, plus employee 401(k) and HSA payroll contributions attributable to that income. The paystub bridge starts with full cash gross and subtracts taxes and required non-saving deductions; report any benefit/noncash and posting residual explicitly. |
| Long-term actual `L` | Posted employee 401(k) + employee HSA + taxable external deposits − distributions/withdrawals to spendable cash; internal long-term account transfers cancel. Show gross components and net, while employer match and returns are context only. `met = L ≥ target` when evidence is complete; otherwise unknown. |
| Unrestricted cash actual `C` | Closing minus opening YNAB `Savings` category balance, adjusted for internal category transfers and corrections, with spending/withdrawals reducing net. `met = C ≥ target` when opening/closing evidence is complete; otherwise unknown. Emergency Fund is a separate stock and top-up flow. |
| Recurring vs irregular | Core targets use `D`; verified bonuses, tax refunds, cashback, and other one-offs appear in a separate received/sourced pool after core target assessment. Expense or HSA reimbursements are return of previously spent/held money and do not increase that pool as new earnings. |

**Recommended decision priority for ETH-622:** show mandatory payroll costs and already-posted 401(k)/HSA contributions first, then actual Needs spending and the protected cash buffer/Emergency Fund position, then unmet unrestricted-cash and long-term-savings minima, then Wants spending and remaining deployable cash. Display Wants over its ceiling as a breach even when covered by irregular income. This is a review order and recommended allocation of *future* surplus; historical lower-priority spend remains visible if a higher-priority stage was unmet. Do not portray modeled gap coverage as an accomplished transfer. The precise order between reserve restoration and additional long-term investing is an owner policy choice for ETH-622; the current Financial Plan prioritized Emergency Fund top-ups differently from the active waterfall.

Set new percentages after classifying historical inflows, validating taxable deposits, and subtracting long-term account withdrawals. For each of the last three complete years, compute Needs and Wants spend, verified net employee long-term additions, and net `Savings` category additions divided by `D`; inspect median and adverse-year capacity, required emergency-fund replenishment, and chosen long-term saving objective. Select spending ceilings and savings minima that are affordable together, with any unallocated margin labeled as buffer. A fixed 100% sum is optional when ceilings and minima have different meanings. The local *candidate* 401(k) + HSA + YNAB taxable-outflow ratios for 2023–2025 are about 17%, 21%, and 36% against `N + 401(k) + HSA`; taxable destination deposits, withdrawal offsets, and recurring income classification remain unverified, so these are calibration inputs, not adopted targets. The Savings category alone had $0, about $240, and $0 net growth in those years; a positive unrestricted-cash minimum represents a deliberate policy change.

Use calendar-year actuals for completed years. For the current year, show YTD contributions against a YTD target derived from recurring eligible income actually received, plus a separately labeled full-year plan/projection. Do not count future budget assignments as received income or achieved saving; do not extrapolate one-off inflows. Show annual target progress and contribution timing separately so a front-loaded retirement contribution can be ahead of YTD pace without implying cash is available for later stages. Show bonuses, refunds, and other irregular inflows separately and allocate verified surplus after core targets; a tax refund or expense reimbursement is not recurring earnings. Split payroll retirement funded by irregular compensation from the core contribution numerator and show it in the irregular allocation path, so one-off income cannot lower the denominator while satisfying a recurring-income target.
